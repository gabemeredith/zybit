The Cobalt family

Eighteen pieces glazed in our house Cobalt — a deep, variegated blue that pools darker in the rim recesses and breaks lighter on the throwing ridges. Cobalt is reduction-fired, which means every piece comes out a slightly different shade.

The Cobalt mug holds twelve ounces, sits comfortably in two hands, and is dishwasher-safe (though we throw it by hand and would rather you washed it by hand).

The Reed family

Sixteen pieces glazed in Reed — a matte, warm-grey green inspired by the dry winter grasses on the Sandy River delta. Reed pieces include four serving bowls, six soup bowls, and a run of small dessert plates.

Reed runs slightly lighter on porcelain bodies than on stoneware, so the dessert plates have a more luminous quality than the bowls — same glaze, different clay.

The Salt-and-Pepper family

Fourteen pieces glazed in our Salt-and-Pepper — a wood-ash glaze with iron specks that read black or rust depending on the kiln atmosphere. The most variable of the three families and our personal favorite.

Salt-and-Pepper this season includes a run of stacking bowls, three larger vessels intended for dry flowers, and a small batch of espresso cups with hand-shaped saucers.

About reduction firing

We fire in a gas reduction kiln, which means partway through the cycle we starve the kiln of oxygen so it pulls oxygen from the glaze itself. This is what produces the depth and surface variation that makes each piece different from the next.

It also means the firing takes thirty-six hours start to finish, and we lose roughly one in twelve pieces to cracks, drips, or glaze defects. The pieces that survive — and ship — are the ones that came through the fire intact.
